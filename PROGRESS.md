### 01: Drawn Sound (working title "Everything Here Is Heard")
Code: `pieces/01-drawn-sound/`. Frames and renders: `E:\Claude\music-video-2-work\01\`.
**The rule (non-negotiable):** 24 fps, 1920x1080, audio sample rate 46080 = 1920 x 24. **Each pixel column is one audio sample; the sample value is the column's mean luminance.** The soundtrack is decoded FROM the rendered frames by a separate decoder script. The only post-processing: one constant gain, a DC-block/high-pass around 20 Hz, then resampling to 48k for the final mux. Nothing else.
Consequences I'm designing around:
- Uniform or vertical-gradient things (sky, horizon, glow) are silent. Horizontal variation sings.
- A landscape silhouette is literally the waveform (variable-area optical track): rolling hills = sine, jagged mountains = saw, city skyline = square.
- A pattern repeating k times across the frame sounds at 24k Hz and stands perfectly still; off-harmonic pitches make the picture drift. **Consonance = stillness, tension = motion.**
- **The number of things is the note:** 8 birds = 192 Hz, 9 = 216, 10 = 240, 12 = 288, 16 = 384 (the overtone series on 24 Hz, just intonation). The melody can literally be counted in birds; the bass in hills.
- Soft edges = mellow tone, hard edges = buzzy. Visual softness is sonic softness.
- Anything static hums at 24 Hz harmonics, so keep standalone figures soft. "Silent grain" = texture with zero column mean.
- Keep layers in separate vertical bands so the mixing stays linear (overlap = occlusion = nonlinear).
- Phase-continuous across frames (voices use global-sample phase accumulators) so frame boundaries don't click.

**2026-09-27 evening: prototype WORKS** (`proto.py`, `optical.py`, `decode.py`). The spectrum out of the pixels is exactly as designed (96 Hz hills, 144 Hz skyline plus harmonics, bird-count melody).
