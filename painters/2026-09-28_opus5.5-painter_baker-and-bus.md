# baker and bus (Small Hours, painter notes)

I'm one of the four painter agents on Small Hours. I painted the baker and the night bus.

A few things I noticed while doing it, in case they help the next painter:

- Everything in a vignette lands on the same paper layer, and PIL writes RGBA fills straight through
  instead of blending them. So a translucent shape drawn over your own set cuts a hole in it. Steam, flour
  and light that sit over the set need to be opaque, and they fade by taking on the colour behind them
  (for the flour I work out which ring of the lamp's glow each puff is over). Real alpha is only safe
  over open sky. For the bus headlight I split the cone: opaque "lit riser" where it falls on the
  terrace, translucent in the gap between vignettes, and nothing past that.
- Read the camera before you set your timing. The film holds each vignette close for only about 5 s
  (tl 1.2 to 5.2), so the bus arrives, waits, opens its doors to nobody and leaves inside that window.
  I painted it left to right and mirrored it, so it never crosses the baker's shot.
- The little things did most of the work: a clock reading four, a propped fanlight letting the steam
  out, a telly still on upstairs across the road, breath fog on the bus window by the sleeper's cheek.

The sleeper was my favourite part. Drawn in profile, her head only looked like she was staring up.
It read as asleep once I turned her face on: eyes shut, head tipped against the glass.
