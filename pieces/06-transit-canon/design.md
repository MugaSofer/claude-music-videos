# Transit Canon: visual design

**Look:** a printed Beck-style transit map on cream paper, as if enamelled on a platform wall
(craft.py `paper` preset). The ink lines are the four line colours. Trains are small lit
lozenges with a soft glow and a fading light-trail. It's fresh against the night palettes of
pieces 2 and 5.

**Every sound has a picture:**
- A train arriving at a station plays its note. The station stamps and pulses (a ring expands
  in the line colour), and its name label brightens and then settles.
- The first lap of each line **lays the track**: the train draws its own ink line behind it, and
  stations are stamped as it first reaches them. The map builds itself from the music.
- Rush-hour trains are dimmer and smaller. When they go out of service, they fade at a station.
- Interchanges are white rings. When two trains share an interchange within 0.3 s, it flashes.
- The convergence at 120 s: all four trains reach Central together. There's a paper-white bloom
  and then a slow ripple that passes over the whole map, each station rippling when the wave
  reaches it.
- As each line leaves in the coda, its ink fades back to a faint pencil ghost. The last image
  is a pale ghost map with violet's final F2 ringing.

**Camera:** the intro starts close on the violet train (zoom 2.6) and pulls back as each
line enters. Rush hour is wide (about 1.0) with a slow drift. The approach from 100 to 120 s is
a slow push to Central (zoom 1.8), easing back after the bloom. The coda pulls out to 0.8 so
the map sits small on the paper with a wide margin.

**Charm:** invented station names in Bahnschrift, e.g. Lantern Row, Low Tide, Quiet Hill,
Night Bakery, Old Signal, Kettle Lane. Central is "Central". There's a legend box in the
corner that fills in line by line as each line enters, like a real map's key.

**Parallel track:** shared unit segments are offset perpendicular to the line so shared
corridors show side-by-side colours, as on a real map.
