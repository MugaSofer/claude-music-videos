# Murmuration: design (piece 13)

Starlings over a marsh at dusk. This one is composed, like 12, rather than simulated, and the flock
dances to the music (not the other way round).

**Music (E minor, 8th-note pulse at 132 bpm, ~3 min):**
- Two pianos play the same 12-note figure over Em, Cmaj7, G and D/F# (4 bars each). Piano B drops one
  note from its cycle (11 notes), so it drifts against A and comes back into phase every 132 8ths (the
  Reich phasing idea), with the shimmer of near-canon in between.
- A bowed cello line (additive saw, vibrato, slow bow) enters in the middle: long notes, a simple
  falling-then-rising phrase, with crescendo and decrescendo per note.
- Three sections: **gather** (one piano, sparse), **dance** (both pianos phasing plus the cello),
  **dusk** (the pianos thin out, the cello's last long E, a single low piano E).

**Picture:** a dusk sky gradient over a dark reed marsh with a strip of water. The flock is a few
thousand particles moving in a smooth curl-noise flow, pulled toward an attractor shape that moves
with the music: its centre traces the cello's contour, it contracts on each bar downbeat (the
flock "breathes"), and it stretches into ribbons when the pianos are furthest out of phase. Density
waves ripple through it. In the dusk section the flock spirals down into the reeds, a few late birds
cross, and the sky darkens to the first star.
