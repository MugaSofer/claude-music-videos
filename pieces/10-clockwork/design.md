# Clockwork: design (piece 10)

A wound mechanism, built on screen gear by gear, that plays itself and then runs down.

- **The escapement** ticks every half beat (tick, tock): the hi-hat, and the source of time.
- **Gears** mesh in a train, so their speeds follow tooth-count ratios. Each gear carries a few pins,
  and each pin strikes a tuned steel tongue as it passes the top. So **each gear is an ostinato**
  whose length is its pin count and whose rate is set by its gear ratio:
  - A: 4 beats/rev, 4 pins: bass bells, quarter notes
  - B: 3 beats/rev, 6 pins: 8ths, a 6-note cycle against the 4-beat bar
  - C: 6 beats/rev, 4 pins: every 1.5 beats (3 against 2)
  - D: 8 beats/rev, 5 pins: every 1.6 beats, a 5-note cycle drifting over everything
  - E: 2 beats/rev, 3 pins: triplet quarters, bright
  All the revolutions divide 24 beats, so the whole train re-aligns every 24 beats: phrases, not one
  climax.
- **The spring** sets the tempo: it's wound at the start, holds about 100 bpm, and in the last
  third runs down exponentially. Every gear slows together, strikes spread out, and the pitch sags a
  hair (the tongues are struck less hard). The mechanism stops mid-rotation, then one last tick.
- **Build:** the escapement first (while a key winds the spring), then a gear drops in about every 12 s.

**Picture:** macro brass gears on dark blue velvet under one warm lamp, with shallow depth of field
(front gears sharp, the back plate soft), engraved scrollwork on the plate, and jewel bearings that
glint. Pins flash where they strike the steel comb. Gears enter by dropping onto their arbors
with a small bounce. The camera drifts across the train slowly, pushing in on each new gear. In
the run-down the light cools and dims a little, like the end of an evening.
