# Passacaglia: design (piece 12)

A direct answer to "a bit simplistic", in the medium Muga liked best (piece 2's piano): a real
piece of piano music with voice-leading, built on an 8-bar ground bass that never changes while ten
variations grow over it.

- **Ground** (D minor, 3/4, 76 bpm, a dotted half per bar): D C Bb A G Bb A D (the descending
  lament tetrachord with a cadence). Harmony: Dm, C, Bb, A, Gm, E°/Bb, A7, Dm.
- **Variations** (about 19 s each):
  1. the ground alone, with a single chord tone ringing per bar
  2. chorale: three voice-led upper voices
  3. broken chords in 8ths
  4. a sung melody (chord tones plus passing tones) over 8ths
  5. canon: the melody imitated an octave lower, one bar behind
  6. running 16ths: scales between chord tones
  7. suspension chain: 4-3 and 7-6 in slow half notes (the dark centre)
  8. triplet arpeggios sweeping the keyboard (building)
  9. climax: full chords, the bass in octaves, the melody in octaves
  10. return: the melody alone, high and soft; the last chord is D *major* (a Picardy third)

**Picture: tree rings.** A slowly turning circle where angle is the position in the 8-bar ground
(the 8 bars are spokes). Each variation draws one new ring outward. Every note is a dot at its angle,
on the ring of its variation, pushed slightly in or out by pitch, so each variation's texture has its
own visible grain (the chorale as beads, the 16ths as fine thread, the triplets as waves). The
ground is the innermost ring, pulsing on each bar. A playhead sweeps round like a clock hand. By the
end it's a dense mandala, a record of the whole piece. Then the Picardy chord turns it all warm gold.
