# Typewriter Weather: design (piece 9)

A letter typed late at night to someone far away. **The typing is the rhythm section**:
keystrokes are quantised to a gentle swung groove, the space bar is the soft kick, the shift key a
rim click, the carriage return a zip plus the "ding" bell at each line end. Each line of the letter
is a bar group with its own chord (a warm electric-piano pad under the typing). **The weather named in
each line becomes a small watercolour vignette** blooming in the margin beside it: rain on a window,
wind in a washing line, fog, sun on a wall, snow, stars. The weather also colours the sound
(rain adds soft noise and droplets; wind a breathy whistle; sun a brighter voicing).

The letter (mine, written for this piece; no copyrighted text):

    Dear you,
    it rained here all morning, the soft kind that forgets to stop.
    by noon the wind had the washing flying like flags.
    then fog came in off the water and took the far houses away.
    at four the sun found one wall and stayed there, warm.
    they say there might be snow tonight. I hope it waits for you.
    the stars are out now, very small, very many.
    I am keeping the weather for you, all of it.
    come home and I will give it back.
    love, C

Picture: a top-down desk at night lit by a lamp. The typewriter platen is at the bottom
edge; the sheet scrolls up as lines are typed (a real typewriter's feed). Type strikes are slightly
uneven in ink density, and the ribbon lightens as it wears. The margin vignettes paint themselves
in wet-on-wet blooms. In the last line the paper is pulled out and folded, and the vignettes sit
together like a strip of weather. Keep it tender, not twee.
