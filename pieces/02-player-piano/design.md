# 02: Player Piano City (design sketch, 2026-09-27)

## Core idea
Conlon Nancarrow spent 40 years in Mexico City punching music by hand into player-piano rolls: music too fast and too tangled for human hands. His signature was the **tempo canon**: the same melody in several voices at *different speeds* (ratios like 3:4:5, even √2:2 or e:π), which drift apart and then **converge** at one pre-calculated instant.

**Mapping: parallax = tempo canon.** The city at night is built of piano-roll strips. Each depth layer of the city is one canon voice, scrolling at its own tempo (farther = slower, like real parallax). The holes punched in each layer's roll are its lit windows; a window sounds as it crosses the "tracker bar", a glowing horizontal line (the street / the tram line?). Pitch = horizontal position (the piano-roll convention), so each building column is a pitch region.
- The canon's **convergence point** = the moment all parallax layers line up exactly, every window stacked, all voices on the same note at once. It's a visual alignment you can *see coming* as the layers slide into register. That's the climax.
- Visually: a dense, glittering night city scrolling upward (the roll feeds up), like looking at skyscrapers from a moving tram. Warm windows on deep blue paper.

## Audio
- Synthesised piano in code (no samples): modal synthesis with stretched partials plus a hammer thump; slightly metallic/prepared (Nancarrow put metal on his hammers, so a hard, bright tone is authentic and forgiving of synthesis).
- Composition: one strong, simple subject (~16 notes), an ostinato bass, and a 3- or 4-voice tempo canon at ratios such as 3:4:5 or 12:15:20, building to convergence at ~70% of the runtime, then a coda of glissandi (Nancarrow's arpeggio sweeps = whole columns of windows lighting in a sweep).
- Length ~2:30.

## Why it's mine / different from piece 1
Piece 1: the picture IS the sound (literally). Piece 2: the *structure* is shared: the time structure (tempo canon) is the space structure (parallax). The rule is a mapping, not an identity. The texture is different too: glittering and dense vs spare and pastoral.

## Open questions
- Scroll direction: upward (roll feeding) vs sideways (tram)? Upward keeps pitch = x cleanly.
- Does the tracker bar read as a thing in the world? Maybe it's a lit tram cable, a river, or simply the horizon glow.
- Honest length: tempo canons are dense; 2:30 may be plenty.
