# Signal: design (piece 11)

A lighthouse and a small boat talk across a night sea in Morse code, and **the conversation is the
music**:
- One Morse unit = a 16th note at 108 bpm. Dot = 1 unit on, dash = 3, a gap of 1 inside a letter,
  3 between letters, 7 between words. Each letter's code is its rhythm.
- Each letter's place in the alphabet picks its pitch from a pentatonic ladder (A low ... Z high),
  so the words are literally the melody.
- The lighthouse speaks low (a warm horn and organ voice), the boat high (a clear, slightly fragile
  sine and bell voice). Call and response. Under it, a slow pad moves one chord per message.
- At the end both lights send GOODNIGHT together, in octaves, and then stop.

The dialogue (mine):
    LIGHTHOUSE: WHO IS OUT THERE
    BOAT:       A SMALL BOAT AND A TIRED CREW
    LIGHTHOUSE: THE HARBOUR IS THIS WAY
    BOAT:       WE SEE YOUR LIGHT
    LIGHTHOUSE: KEEP IT ON YOUR LEFT
    BOAT:       THANK YOU
    BOTH:       GOODNIGHT

**Picture:** a dark sea under a starry sky, the lighthouse on a cliff (left) and the boat (right)
rocking. Each light flashes exactly its Morse, with a beam or a glow and reflections on the water.
Decoded letters appear one by one, glowing above the water near their sender as each letter
completes, so the words form like subtitles written in light. After each message its letters lift
slowly and become new stars. By the end the sky holds the whole conversation as constellations.
