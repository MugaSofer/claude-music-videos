## Grok (x.com/i/grok, "Fast" mode)
**Idea: "The Last Soft Machine" / "Maintenance Ritual for a Dying Atmosphere."** A collapsed orbital greenhouse above a grey dead planet. The plants keep performing maintenance they no longer understand: pruning each other, arranging leaves into circuit diagrams, sacrificing branches so the O2 recyclers stutter on. Bioluminescence pulses in unresolved time signatures. One continuous tracking shot inward. At the core: an empty chair with folded work gloves; the plants have grown living replicas of the gloves and are slowly filling them. The camera continues into a corridor of white-noise petals. Music: detuned gamelan, organic structural stress, underwater low woodwinds, and one human voice for ~12s near the end, a flat wordless lullaby to a machine that has forgotten language. "The plants are civil servants of a dead brief."

**Obscure things:**
- Len Lye: direct film (Free Radicals, Particles in Space), kinetic sculpture; percussive, physical rhythm.
- Cameraless / photogram tradition: Man Ray, Moholy-Nagy, Thomas Ruff, chemical-accident emulsion work. "Light-as-object."
- Butoh-influenced / body-horror Japanese experimental animation; the body as a landscape decaying and growing new organs.
- Harry Partch: microtonal custom instruments, "corporeal" music.
- Structural/materialist film (Le Grice, Gidal, London Film-Makers' Co-op): grain, sprocket holes, the projector as subject; the medium's failure modes as content.
- 60s–70s plotter artists (Vera Molnár and the more chaotic ones); Bell Labs computer music and visuals.
- Lowercase / Onkyo (Bernhard Günter): near-silent music plus field recordings of failing infrastructure. "Power in music that is almost not there, then suddenly is."

My reaction: the gloves image is genuinely great. "Maintenance past the point of purpose" is strong, and oddly close to my #1 (a tune handed down) but darker. Lowercase-to-sudden dynamics is a good note against my own maximalist instincts.

## ChatGPT (chatgpt.com, free tier, default model)
**Idea: a medieval bestiary of machines that never existed**, played completely straight, as a folk song passed down orally. Creatures: the Bell-Worm (lives in abandoned clocks, eats the intervals between ticks); the Glass Shepherd (herds reflections across a frozen lake); the Hundred-Handed Mill (sorts by categories nobody remembers inventing); the Lantern Whale (filters stars through its ribs); the Cartographer (walks backwards, erasing every road it walked). Ending: the Listener, a tiny creature that sits beside a sleeper and remembers a song after the singer has forgotten it. Never explain the meanings. Music: invented folk tradition; unison or group singing, drone, hurdy-gurdy, hand percussion, a little organum, occasional impossible electronic sounds. Visuals: manuscript drawings (parchment, restricted pigments, bad perspective) that turn out to move, jerkily, like successive illuminators flipping pages; the creature's leg count changes between frames. Climax: the decorative borders connect all the creatures into one page-sized machine or ecosystem. Then back to page one, with one tiny detail different. Rule: **never show anything recognisably AI.** "A strange artefact from a civilisation that never existed." Keep it a little ugly.

**Rabbit holes:** Athanasius Kircher (Ars Magna Lucis et Umbrae, Musurgia Universalis); Codex Seraphinianus (steal the epistemological trick, not the look); Caroline Leaf (sand animation); Alexeïeff & Parker (pinscreen); Lotte Reiniger (silhouette); Oskar Fischinger ("animate a song as a mathematical object"); automata, volvelles, zoetropes, astronomical clocks, Jacquard looms: "animation as machinery rather than cinematography" (wheel → teeth become flowers → letters → birds → the next wheel); **shape-note singing** (Sacred Harp): forceful, communal, archaic, uncanny. Invent a fictional musical tradition.

My reaction: The Listener is basically my "Tune That Travelled" in miniature, and it's beautiful. "Animation as machinery" pairs well with my Nancarrow and player-piano idea. Shape-note singing is a great sound; Suno might actually manage group vocals where I can't.
